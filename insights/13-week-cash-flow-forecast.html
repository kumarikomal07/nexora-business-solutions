Financial Planning

Building a 13-week cash flow forecast that leadership will actually use

Nexora Insights30 September 20266 min read

A rolling 13-week forecast is one of the simplest and most powerful finance tools available. Here is how to build one that drives decisions rather than sitting in a folder.

Profit is an opinion; cash is a fact. For growing and changing businesses, a rolling 13-week cash flow forecast is often the single most useful finance tool, giving leaders a clear view of the next quarter at weekly resolution. Done well, it becomes a weekly management habit rather than a spreadsheet exercise.

Why 13 weeks?

Thirteen weeks covers a full quarter, which is long enough to see payroll cycles, VAT payments, rent and seasonal swings, and short enough to forecast with reasonable accuracy. It complements, rather than replaces, your annual budget and longer-term financial model.

Build it from receipts and payments, not the P&L

A direct cash forecast lists actual expected receipts and payments by week. Start with opening bank balances, then forecast customer receipts based on real debtor ageing and payment behaviour, followed by payroll, suppliers, tax, rent, loan repayments and capital spend. Keep the categories few and meaningful.

Make ownership clear

The forecast is only as good as the inputs. Give named owners responsibility for their lines: sales for major receipts, operations for supplier commitments, HR for payroll changes. A short weekly call to update and challenge the numbers is usually enough.

Compare forecast to actual every week

Variance analysis is where the learning happens. Tracking last week's forecast against what actually happened improves accuracy quickly and highlights where processes, such as credit control, need attention.

Use it to decide, not just to report

The real value comes when the forecast shapes action: chasing particular debtors, timing a hire, negotiating supplier terms or opening a conversation with a lender early. A forecast that flags a pinch point eight weeks out gives you options. One that flags it next week gives you very few.
