How to Write a Board-Ready Business Case

26 August 20264 min readNexora Insights team

Boards approve decisions, not documents. A clear structure helps your investment proposal get the right answer, faster.

Many good ideas are delayed or rejected not because they lack merit, but because the case for them is hard to follow. A board-ready business case makes the decision easy to understand and easy to challenge.

Lead with the decision

State on the first page exactly what you are asking for: the investment, the timeline and the outcome. Directors should know within a minute what they are being asked to approve.

Show the options you rejected

Present at least three options, including “do nothing”, with costs, benefits and risks for each. This shows rigour and pre-empts the most common board question: “what else did you consider?”

Be honest about risk

  • List the key assumptions behind the numbers.
  • Show what happens to returns if the main assumptions are wrong.
  • Explain how the risks will be monitored and who owns them.

Define success up front

Agree the measures and review points that will show whether the investment is working. This builds trust and makes the next request easier to approve.
